01
The problem

Graduates leave strong in skill, short on the human capabilities

Industry keeps naming the same gap: not technical skill, but collaboration, adaptability, and handling critique. As AI commoditises the technical, these become a graduate’s durable value. Yet they form in interaction across a whole degree, and assessment reads the final artefact, so they stay invisible.

This is a learning problem before an employability one: relational capability develops slowly, unevenly, and off the transcript.
Grounded in: programmatic assessment (Heeneman et al. 2021); career development learning (Bridgstock 2009) · Future Readiness & Professional Learning survey, Sem 1 2026 (n=17) · COMM2584 internship supervisor feedback, Sem 3 2025

02b
The evidence · interviews + literature

The capability that matters happens between people

Design-based research: secondary data plus three interviews — Christian, Tom, and my mentor Hellmut. No student data was collected; as their assessor, interviewing students directly would compromise both ethics and validity.

Christian · Professional Practice + Internship

“They take critique very personal... they feel hurt... quite vulnerable to critique.”

From years of internship logs, not impression.

Tom · industry studio owner + educator

“A CV is just paperwork... they lie so much on it.”

He trusts what a person actually does, not what they claim.

Documented in design education: critique exposes not only ideas but identities, which makes feedback emotionally charged and helps explain why students defend rather than absorb it (Schön 1983; design-education critique research). A transcript records the finished object; it cannot record how a student worked with others, or how they grew.

‘Trace of learning’: an informal, not-yet-formalised indicator of learning in interaction, distinct from formal assessment (informal-learning literature; Colley, Hodkinson & Malcolm 2003). A trace cannot be performed for, because it comes from real practice. The identity question is grounded in professional identity formation research (Trede, Macklin & Bridges 2012).

A working prototype

A system, shown through a prototype tool: the leave-a-trace then deepen mechanic, the identity reflection, a threshold gate, and a shareable archive. The AI assistant asks a question but never scores, so judgement stays with the student (guarding against metacognitive laziness, Fan et al. 2025).

08b
The prototype, step by step

The threshold, and the archive

Only deepened traces count toward the threshold, reachable in-school by every student.
The archive is the evidence students write from, and the link they can share with a mentor or employer. Each deepened trace keeps its capability tag, access category, and the identity reflection that made it count.
08c
Findings · what testing showed

The design was sharpened, not simply confirmed

  • Problem confirmed, triangulated: survey plus all three interviews, independently (Christian, Tom, Hellmut)
  • Micro-credential → point → pathway → practice: the model works for technical skill, but not the relational gap PACE targets
  • Assessment is workable: via Program Learning Outcomes and an alternative-assignment route, not points on a rubric (Hellmut), grounded in programmatic assessment (van der Vleuten et al. 2018)
  • Trust: industry believes verifiable participation, not self-report (Tom)
  • Adaptability may lead the other two capabilities (Tom)
  • Not a duplicate: RMIT’s Personal Edge is co-curricular, voluntary, and uncredited — PACE is embedded, credit-bearing, and reaches every student, not just the self-selected few

09
For discussion

Three questions I’d like to think through with you

  • The foundations: verified participation, not self-report, is what makes a trace trustworthy in the design. Is that enough on its own, or does real industry trust require RMIT to build ongoing, formal industry collaboration into the system, not just design it in?
  • The process: the PLO and alternative-assignment route make this workable on paper. But who actually owns it across the three years and five courses, from Foundation to Exhibits, and what happens when a course coordinator changes?
  • The bigger view: if this became how the School evidences its graduates, what would it demand of RMIT as an institution, and of the accreditation and government bodies that decide what a qualification is allowed to certify?
